HIGHSIGHT FINANCIAL — NARRATIVE DESIGN DOCUMENT
================================================

SETTING
-------
Highsight Financial exists outside of time. It is an institution that trades on
already-happened markets — not to profit, but to train. The player does not know
why they are here or what the endgame is. That mystery is the long-term hook.

The tone is: cozy trading life sim (90%) with slow-building uncanny dread (10%).
Think Stardew Valley meets The Stanley Parable in a corporate liminal space.


CORE CHARACTERS
---------------

THE PLAYER
- Cannot remember how they arrived.
- Was "selected" — not randomly.
- Starts as a new hire on Floor 1.
- Progression through trading skill IS narrative progression.

THE PRINCIPAL
- Runs Highsight Financial.
- Features never resolve visually — silhouetted or blurred, not shadowed.
- Voice is warm but wrong in a way you can't name.
- Appears rarely. Always knows more than they should.
- Never threatening. Always accommodating. That's what's unsettling.

CASEY (Mentor)
- Friendly, slightly harried, clearly human.
- Genuinely believes in teaching the player.
- Works on Floor 1. Has never been past Floor 3.
- Serves as tutorial guide and recurring friendly face.
- Does NOT know the institution's true nature.


SEQUENCE OF EVENTS
------------------

1. MAIN MENU
   - Player model stands on a small floating platform (fragment of sidewalk,
     a piece of the "real world" suspended in void).
   - Ambient city sounds, distant and muffled.
   - On pressing Play: platform crumbles. Player falls silently, weightlessly
     into a spiraling black hole. Screen compresses to a point of light.

2. THE ARRIVAL (Clouds Scene)
   - Explodes outward into: a sea of luminous clouds.
   - Slow-drifting constellations that don't match any real sky.
   - Player stands on a glass-like surface with no visible bottom.
   - In the far distance: a structure — angular, impossibly tall, faintly
     glowing at its edges. Looks like a skyscraper designed by someone who
     only had one described to them.
   - Player internal monologue (dialogue boxes):
     "..."
     "Where is this?"
     "I was just... I was on my way to..."
     "...I can't remember."
   - Player walks toward the building. No other direction exists.
   - As they approach, details resolve: glass doors, reception desk, warm light.
     It looks almost normal. Almost welcoming.

3. THE HIRING (Lobby)
   - Pristine, too-clean lobby. Marble that seems to hum. Single elevator.
   - No receptionist. Elevator opens on its own.
   - Top floor: a room. Backlit. A desk. The Principal sits behind it.
   - The Principal speaks:
     "You're early. That's good. We value punctuality at Highsight."
     "You've been selected. Not randomly — we don't do random here."
     "Your position begins immediately. Floor 1. Your mentor is waiting."
     "Oh — and welcome to Highsight Financial. You'll do well here.
      Everyone does."
   - No interview. No explanation. No option to refuse.
   - Elevator takes player down.

4. CASEY'S TUTORIAL (Floor 1 — The Bullpen)
   - Doors open to something warmer: soft lighting, plants, hum of work.
   - Casey turns and waves.
   - Casey dialogue:
     "Hey! New hire, right? Don't worry, everyone has that look their
      first day."
     "I'm Casey. I'll be showing you the ropes. Come on, let me show
      you your desk."

   Casey teaches three things:

   A) THE DAY CYCLE
      "We work in phases here. Pre-market is when you plan. Market hours
       are when things move. Post-market is when you see how you did.
       Then you rest. Simple rhythm."

   B) THE KNOWLEDGE LATTICE (their name for the knowledge graph)
      "See this? It's your progression map. As you trade and learn, new
       concepts unlock. Some you study yourself. Others... find you,
       when you're ready."
      [Player sees first 3 nodes: What is a Company > What is a Stock >
       Market Buy/Sell]

   C) FIRST TRADE
      "Don't overthink it. Your first trade is never your best.
       That's the point."
      [Guided walkthrough: select ticker, set quantity, queue buy order,
       open markets]

5. DAILY LOOP BEGINS
   - After tutorial, the normal game cycle starts.
   - Player has freedom: trade, explore Floor 1, check lattice.
   - Casey remains available for optional dialogue hints.


POST-TUTORIAL NARRATIVE BEATS
-----------------------------

WEEK 1 COMPLETE:
- Casey mentions other floors.
  "You'll get access eventually. Everyone moves up."
- Player notices an elevator button that's grayed out.

FIRST ADAPTIVE NODE UNLOCK (e.g., Volatility):
- Casey is NOT the one who explains it.
- A typed note appears on the player's desk. No signature.
- The knowledge content feels different — more clinical.

AFTER 10+ TRADING DAYS:
- Player spots another new hire in the lobby.
- They have the same confused look.
- The Principal greets them with the exact same words.

LATE GAME (Floor 3+):
- Higher floors unlock as player masters concepts.
- Trading gets harder. Knowledge graph branches into territory Casey
  doesn't understand.
- Casey: "I've never been past Floor 3. I don't know what's up there."

ENDGAME (not yet designed):
- The player's mastery of markets reveals something about what Highsight
  actually is.
- The Principal's true purpose becomes clear.
- Leave open for now — the mystery sustains engagement.


DESIGN PRINCIPLES
-----------------

1. LEARNING IS THE PLOT
   The player's progression through trading concepts IS their narrative
   progression through the building. Every floor corresponds to deeper
   financial knowledge.

2. NEVER BREAK THE COZY
   The uncanny elements are background radiation, not jump scares. The
   player should feel safe to learn and experiment. Dread is seasoning,
   not the main course.

3. SHOW, DON'T GATE
   Narrative beats are triggered by player actions (trades, node completions,
   days elapsed) — never by forced cutscenes that block gameplay. The player
   can always choose to keep trading.

4. THE INSTITUTION IS THE ANTAGONIST (MAYBE)
   Keep it ambiguous. Highsight might be benevolent (genuinely training
   people). It might be exploitative (using trained traders for something).
   Don't resolve this early. Let players theorize.

5. CASEY IS REAL
   Casey is not a plant. She genuinely cares. If the institution turns out
   to be sinister, Casey is as much a victim as the player. Protect this
   character's authenticity.

KNOWLEDGE LATTICE — FIRST 3 NODES (Tutorial)
---------------------------------------------

Node 1: "What is a Company"
  - Casey explains: businesses, ownership, why companies exist.
  - Unlocked at game start (root node).

Node 2: "What is a Stock"
  - Casey explains: shares, ownership stakes, why prices move.
  - Unlocked after completing Node 1.

Node 3: "Market Buy / Sell"
  - Casey walks player through their first actual trade.
  - Unlocked after completing Node 2.
  - Completing this node ends the tutorial and begins free play.


DIALOGUE STYLE GUIDE
---------------------

- Casey: Casual, warm, uses contractions. Slight humor. Never condescending.
  Example: "Yeah, I lost money my first week too. Everyone does. The trick
  is figuring out WHY you lost it."

- The Principal: Formal but not stiff. Short sentences. Declarative. No
  questions — only statements. Unnerving politeness.
  Example: "You'll understand in time. Everyone does."

- Player (internal): Fragmented. Trailing off. Uncertain. Gets more
  confident as days pass.
  Example early: "I don't... why can't I remember?"
  Example later: "Something about this place doesn't add up."

- Typed Notes (anonymous): Clinical. Precise. Educational but cold.
  No warmth. Pure information delivery.
  Example: "Volatility measures the degree of variation in price over time.
  High volatility implies high risk. You experienced this today."
