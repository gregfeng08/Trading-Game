HIGHSIGHT FINANCIAL — THESIS NARRATIVE & FRAMING
=================================================
Created: 2026-05-26


UNIFIED THESIS
--------------
Static financial education fails because students memorize definitions without
building intuition. Trading simulators improve on textbooks but remain
fundamentally static — same lessons, same feedback, same content regardless of
what the student actually does. Highsight Financial bridges this gap by
combining three mutually reinforcing design pillars:

  1. GAMIFICATION as the WHY — Embodied experience creates durable memory.
     A student who lives through the 2008 financial crisis in-game remembers
     what a flash crash feels like, not just what the textbook says. The arc
     system, knowledge graph, and day cycle create a Stardew Valley-inspired
     rhythm that makes learning feel like play.

  2. LLM-GENERATED CONTENT as the HOW — Dynamic content is what separates
     this from every other trading sim. The newspaper contextualizes the market
     conditions the player is actually trading in. NPC dialogue reacts to the
     player's portfolio and decisions. The adaptive knowledge graph triggers
     educational content precisely when the player's behavior makes it relevant
     (e.g., a lesson on stop-losses surfaces after the player experiences a
     preventable loss). No two players receive the same educational experience.

  3. SERVER-AUTHORITATIVE ARCHITECTURE as the WHAT MAKES IT POSSIBLE — The
     server holds trading history, portfolio state, knowledge progress, and
     market context. This state is what feeds into LLM prompts to generate
     contextually relevant content. The architecture is not a feature — it is
     the infrastructure that connects gamification to adaptivity.

These are not three separate contributions. They are three layers of one
system: the game creates experiences, the server tracks them, and the LLM
makes them pedagogically meaningful.

THE CENTRAL CONTRIBUTION
------------------------
The reactive knowledge graph is the thesis's crown jewel. It is the mechanism
by which gamification and LLM adaptivity converge:

  - Traditional approach: Student reads Chapter 5 on risk management after
    finishing Chapter 4. Linear. Context-free. Forgettable.

  - Highsight approach: Student over-concentrates their portfolio in one stock.
    The knowledge graph detects this via the "portfolio_concentration" trigger.
    A node on diversification unlocks. The content explains exactly what the
    player did and why it matters. The lesson arrives at the moment of maximum
    relevance.

This is constructivist learning (Piaget) operationalized through game
mechanics — the student constructs understanding from their own experience,
and the system ensures the right scaffolding appears at the right time.

The 11 behavioral triggers currently implemented:
  - trade_count              (trading activity threshold)
  - held_stock_daily_change  (experiencing price movement)
  - portfolio_concentration  (over-allocation risk)
  - first_profitable_sell    (experiencing profit)
  - first_losing_sell        (experiencing loss)
  - same_ticker_multiple_buys (averaging down/up behavior)
  - unrealized_gain_pct      (paper gains awareness)
  - bought_after_decline     (buy-the-dip behavior)
  - low_cash_ratio           (cash management)
  - market_wide_decline      (systemic risk exposure)
  - traded_both_phases       (market timing awareness)

Each trigger maps to a real pedagogical moment. This is not gamification for
engagement — it is gamification as curriculum delivery.


HOW THE LLM LAYER REINFORCES THIS
----------------------------------
The LLM-generated content serves three functions:

  1. NEWSPAPER ("The Market Tribune")
     - Generated per game-day using Claude API
     - System prompt includes: arc narrative tone, real historical events for
       the date, market movers (top gainers/losers), player's portfolio context
     - Output: headline + articles that contextualize the market the player is
       trading in
     - Purpose: Immersion. The player reads a newspaper that describes the
       world they are living in, not a static flavor text blob. This makes
       historical market events feel present-tense and personal.

  2. NPC DIALOGUE
     - Dynamic dialogue generated based on: current phase, player's recent
       trades, portfolio state, market conditions, NPC personality type
       (analyst, broker, trader, anchor)
     - Four personality archetypes produce different perspectives on the same
       market events
     - Purpose: Multiple viewpoints. A student hears a bullish broker and a
       cautious analyst react to the same crash differently. This models the
       real-world information environment where investors must synthesize
       conflicting opinions.

  3. ADAPTIVE KNOWLEDGE CONTENT
     - When a behavioral trigger fires, the unlocked node's content can
       reference what the player actually did
     - Purpose: Personalized instruction. The system doesn't just say
       "diversification is important" — it says "you had 80% of your portfolio
       in one stock when it dropped 12%."


NARRATIVE FLOW FOR DEMO (June 2)
---------------------------------
The demo should walk through one complete game day to show all three pillars
in action:

  1. PRE-MARKET PHASE
     - Player is in the Room. Walk to terminal.
     - Show the newspaper for today's date (LLM-generated, contextual).
     - Queue a buy order. Show the order in the pending queue.
     - Talking point: "The newspaper is generated by Claude API using real
       historical events and the player's portfolio context."

  2. DAY PHASE
     - Transition to City. Timer counting down.
     - NPCs walking around (leyline pathfinding).
     - Walk near an NPC — show dynamic dialogue about market conditions.
     - Visit the knowledge graph terminal. Show current state: some locked,
       some unlocked, some completed.
     - Talking point: "Every element in this city responds to the player's
       trading behavior and the current market context."

  3. POST-MARKET PHASE ("the second before market closes")
     - Timer expires or player returns to Room.
     - Daily summary overlay: trades executed, P&L, market movers.
     - Player can react to results by queuing and confirming orders at the
       close price (same batch/confirm flow as pre-market, fills same day).
     - A knowledge graph trigger fires (e.g., first_profitable_sell after
       a winning trade). Notification appears.
     - Open knowledge graph — show the newly unlocked node with contextual
       content.
     - Talking point: "The system detected what the player experienced and
       surfaced the relevant educational content at that exact moment."

  4. ARC CONTEXT
     - Show the DayCycleHUD: current arc, days remaining, projected grade.
     - Briefly explain arc grading and cash multiplier consequences.
     - Talking point: "Arcs create narrative progression and stakes. A D grade
       in the bull market means starting the crisis arc with less capital."

WHY THIS MATTERS
-----------------
The argument is not "games are better than textbooks" — that's been made.
The argument is: "What happens when you combine game-based experiential
learning with generative AI that makes every experience pedagogically
unique?" That intersection is underexplored. Most educational games have
static content. Most LLM education tools lack embodied experience. Highsight
Financial sits at the intersection: the game creates the experience, and the
AI makes the experience teach.
