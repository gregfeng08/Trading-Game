Wall Street Rings in a New Decade With Broad Rally

Market Recap
Markets opened the first trading day of 2010 with broad-based optimism as investors bet on continued recovery from the financial crisis. The S&P 500 closed above 1,130, its highest level since October 2008, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 1.5% on strong volume. Banking and technology stocks led the charge, with JPMorgan rising 2.8% and Apple climbing 1.6% on renewed consumer confidence.
markets
Investors Enter 2010 Cautiously Optimistic After Turbulent Decade
After a decade bookended by the dot-com bust and the worst financial crisis since the Great Depression, investors are entering 2010 with cautious hope. The S&P 500 gained 23% in 2009, clawing back much of the ground lost in the collapse, but unemployment remains stubbornly near 10%. Analysts warn that the recovery, while real, remains fragile and dependent on continued government support.
economy
Fed Holds Rates Near Zero as Recovery Takes Shape
The Federal Reserve enters 2010 with its benchmark rate pinned between 0% and 0.25%, where it has sat since December 2008. Chairman Bernanke has signaled that rates will remain “exceptionally low” for an “extended period,” giving markets a clear runway. The question for investors is not whether the recovery will continue, but how quickly the Fed will need to begin tightening — and whether it will act too late.
technology
Apple Rumors Swirl: Tablet Device Expected Within Weeks
Speculation continues to mount that Apple will announce a tablet computer at a widely anticipated event later this month. Industry sources suggest the device, rumored to be called the “iPad,” will sit between the iPhone and MacBook in Apple’s lineup. With Apple shares already up over 140% from their 2009 low, investors are watching closely to see whether the device can open an entirely new product category or whether expectations have already outpaced reality.
